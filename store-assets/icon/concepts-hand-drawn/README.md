# Hand-drawn icon concepts

These five preview-only concepts do not replace the current Android launcher icon.

- **A — Stacked plans:** overlapping route sheets communicate multiple saved plans; the front arrow marks the active step.
- **B — Layered routes:** several paths connect two portals; the teal path is the currently guided link.
- **C — Guidance compass:** planned links converge into a navigation needle, emphasizing guidance.
- **D — Folded plan:** a hand-drawn planning map carries several portal routes and one active direction.
- **E — Link queue:** a sequence of portal links plus previous/next chevrons directly reflects the floating-window controls.

## Prompt set

Common brief used for every concept:

> Use case: logo-brand. Asset type: Android app icon concept. Create an original hand-drawn minimalist line-art logo for a mobile overlay app that guides a user through multiple portal-link plans step by step. Use imperfect hand-inked monoline drawing, subtly irregular human strokes, a flat two-color mark, and highly simplified vector-friendly shapes. Center one symbol inside a simple rounded-square app-icon field with generous safe margin and a strong silhouette readable at 48 px and under circular Android masks. Use a warm off-white background, deep navy ink, and one restrained teal accent for the currently guided step. Symbol only: no words, letters, numbers, labels, screenshots, gradients, shadows, 3D, photorealism, mockups, watermarks, third-party game logos, location-pin clichés, or decorative clutter. Exactly one self-contained icon, not a contact sheet.

Concept-specific additions:

- **A:** Three slightly offset hand-drawn route cards or map sheets, each containing a tiny pair of portal circles joined by a line; the front sheet has one clear forward arrow. The overlapping layers must immediately suggest several plans, while the front arrow suggests current-step guidance.
- **B:** Two portal rings connected by three parallel, gently arcing hand-drawn paths like layered planning alternatives; one middle path becomes a teal arrow toward the destination. Keep the geometry sparse and unmistakable.
- **C:** Form a loose compass or navigation needle entirely from three converging hand-drawn route lines and three small portal rings; one teal directional segment leads forward while the other two navy lines imply additional planned links. Avoid a conventional map pin.
- **D:** A single minimal hand-drawn folded map or accordion sheet containing three simple linked portal paths, with one teal chevron advancing along the foremost path. Make the fold and multiple planned routes legible with very few strokes.
- **E:** A vertical or diagonal sequence of four small hand-drawn portal rings joined as several planned links, framed by two subtle opposing navigation chevrons for previous and next; emphasize one current link in teal. The mark should communicate a queue of links being followed step by step.
